1 · How we score artists

Every artist carries a handful of labels — doom, melodic, hardcore punk, 2000s — collected from MusicBrainz and Last.fm. The ten or so artists you name give us your labels. A percentage is simply how much an artist on the bill shares them.

Nothing is needed from your listening history, which is why a band nobody has heard of can be scored just as well as a famous one. It is a measure of overlap, not a promise that you will like them.

2 · How “rising” is measured

39,778 bookings across 36 festivals and 331 editions. We track the share of festivals an artist is booked at, year on year. Climbing is rising.

This describes the past, not the future. The badge only appears where there is enough history to measure one — three years, three festivals — which is a minority of any lineup.

3 · How your schedule is built

Your day is built from three things: the artists you named, recommended artists who match your taste, and breaks so you can pace yourself. The scheduler fits all three into the best day available to you.

It allows for breaks, meals, the walk between stages and the daily limit you set — and none of it is final: change any set and the day is worked out again around it.

What the numbers on screen mean

43% match How many of that artist's labels are also on the artists you named. It measures overlap, not how much you will enjoy them.
Your pick One of the artists you named. No percentage is shown, because an artist always matches themselves perfectly.
Rising Booked at a growing share of festivals, over at least three years and three festivals. Most of a lineup has too little history to say, and then nothing is claimed.
Chosen over Korn The set this one beat for the slot. Taking the other instead is one click, and the schedule says first what it would cost you.
Joined 10 min late You are walking from the last set. The time shown is when you get there, not when the band starts.
No close match Nothing playing then looks like your taste. The slot is filled with the best of what is on rather than left empty.

Where the data comes from

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata festival lineups
  • MusicBrainz artist metadata and tags
  • Last.fm tags
  • setlist.fm set times

Every row records where it came from and what its licence allows. 15,799 artists are in the corpus.

What this cannot do

  • An artist with one appearance has no trajectory.
  • Tag quality varies a lot for very small artists.
  • Rest guidance is advisory, not medical.
  • A match percentage is tag overlap, not a prediction.

The 2027 lineups on this site are demonstration data, entered by hand. The historical lineups they are measured against are real.
