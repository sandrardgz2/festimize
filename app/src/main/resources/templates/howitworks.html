1 · How we score artists

Every artist carries a handful of labels — doom, melodic, hardcore punk, 2000s — collected from MusicBrainz and Last.fm. The ten or so artists you name give us your labels. A percentage is simply how much an artist on the bill shares them.

Nothing is needed from your listening history, which is why a band nobody has heard of can be scored just as well as a famous one. It is a measure of overlap, not a promise that you will like them.

2 · How “rising” is measured

39,778 bookings across 36 festivals and 331 editions. We track the share of festivals an artist is booked at, year on year. Climbing is rising.

This describes the past, not the future. The badge only appears where there is enough history to measure one — three years, three festivals — which is a minority of any lineup.

3 · How your schedule is built

Filling every minute is easy, and not much use. The schedule picks the sets that suit you best while leaving time to eat, sit down and get from one stage to the next. It works the day out all at once rather than a set at a time, because taking the best band on at noon can cost you two better ones at six — every way the day could fit together is compared, and the best one wins.

It keeps five rules: never two sets at the same time, a break every few hours, time to eat, nine hours between the end of one night and the start of the next morning, and a daily limit you set yourself. It also knows you have to walk — if a band finishes across the field, you join the next one a few minutes late rather than teleporting.

None of it is final. You can drop a set, take the one it beat instead, add anything on the bill, move your meal, or catch the end of something in a gap — and the day is worked out again around whatever you decide.

Does it actually work?

Several definitions of “rising” are defensible. This one was chosen by holding back the last three years of the corpus, computing each definition from the years before them, and asking which picked the artists who were in fact booked more afterwards. 669 artists were eligible, after holding out the 1,073 already in the top tenth — a rising badge should mean ascending, and Metallica being booked a lot is not news.

Trend line finds more than twice as many of the artists who went on to be booked heavily as picking at random would. It does not beat simple recency on rank correlation, and moving up the bill — the measure originally designed — was tested and dropped: at 0.079 it carried nothing that knowing an artist played lately did not already carry. Where the data comes from

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata festival lineups
  • MusicBrainz artist metadata and tags
  • Last.fm tags
  • setlist.fm set times

Every row records where it came from and what its licence allows. 15,799 artists are in the corpus.

What this cannot do

  • An artist with one appearance has no trajectory.
  • Tag quality varies a lot for very small artists.
  • Rest guidance is advisory, not medical.
  • A match percentage is tag overlap, not a prediction.

The 2027 lineups on this site are demonstration data, entered by hand. The historical lineups they are measured against are real.
