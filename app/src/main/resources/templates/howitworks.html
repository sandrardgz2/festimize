1 · How we score artists

The artists you name become a tag profile. Every artist on the lineup is tagged from MusicBrainz and Last.fm, then compared to it.

No listening data is needed, so an artist nobody has heard of can still be scored. A percentage is the overlap between their tags and yours — not a prediction that you will like them.

2 · How “rising” is measured

39,778 bookings across 36 festivals and 331 editions. We track the share of festivals an artist is booked at, year on year. Climbing is rising.

This describes the past, not the future. The badge only appears where there is enough history to measure one — three years, three festivals — which is a minority of any lineup.

3 · How your schedule is built

The aim is the most music you can actually sustain, not the most music. Every day is solved exactly rather than greedily: each set asks whether the day is better with it or without it.

Rules: no overlapping sets · a break every few hours · a meal window · nine hours between the end of one day and the start of the next · a daily cap you set yourself.

Does it actually work?

Several definitions of “rising” are defensible. This one was chosen by holding back the last three years of the corpus, computing each definition from the years before them, and asking which picked the artists who were in fact booked more afterwards. 669 artists were eligible, after holding out the 1,073 already in the top tenth — a rising badge should mean ascending, and Metallica being booked a lot is not news.

Trend line finds more than twice as many of the artists who went on to be booked heavily as picking at random would. It does not beat simple recency on rank correlation, and moving up the bill — the measure originally designed — was tested and dropped: at 0.079 it carried nothing that knowing an artist played lately did not already carry. The full method is in docs/evaluation/momentum-backtest.md.

Where the data comes from

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata festival lineups
  • MusicBrainz artist metadata and tags
  • Last.fm tags
  • setlist.fm set times

Every row records where it came from and what its licence allows. 15,799 artists are in the corpus.

What this cannot do

  • An artist with one appearance has no trajectory.
  • Tag quality varies a lot for very small artists.
  • Rest guidance is advisory, not medical.
  • A match percentage is tag overlap, not a prediction.

The 2027 lineups on this site are demonstration data, entered by hand. The historical lineups they are measured against are real.
